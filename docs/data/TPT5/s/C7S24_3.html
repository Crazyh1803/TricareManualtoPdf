Medicine
Chapter 7
Section 24.3
Coverage Of Cancer Clinical Trials On And After August 27, 2025
Issue Date:  March 4, 2026
Authority:  32 CFR 199.4(e)(26)
Revision:  C-45, March 5, 2026
1.0  Description
The Department of Defense (DoD) and the National Institutes of Health (NIH) entered into a new interagency agreement regarding coverage of NIH-sponsored and approved clinical trials on September 18, 2020; this agreement includes National Cancer Institute (NCI) cancer clinical trials. DoD published a Final Rule (90 FR 35422) effective August 27, 2025, which expands coverage for cancer clinical trials. See Section 24.1 for coverage requirements for NCI Cancer Clinical trials prior to August 27, 2025.
2.0  POLICY
2.1  Coverage of services and supplies associated with cancer clinical trial participation is authorized for those TRICARE-eligible patients selected to participate in NCI-sponsored or approved Phase I, Phase II, Phase Ill, and Phase IV clinical trials studying the prevention, screening, early detection, and treatment of cancer. TRICARE will cost-share all medical care and testing required to determine eligibility for an NCI-sponsored or approved clinical trial, including the evaluation for eligibility at the institution conducting the NCI-sponsored or approved clinical trial. TRICARE will also cost-share all medical care required as a result of participation in NCI-sponsored or approved clinical trials, unless otherwise excluded in this section or in 32 CFR 199.4(e)(26).
2.2  This includes purchasing and administering all approved chemotherapy agents (except for sponsor-funded investigational agents), all inpatient and outpatient care, including diagnostic and laboratory services not otherwise reimbursed under an NCI grant program or provided in the NIH Clinical Center, if the following conditions are met:
2.2.1  The provider seeking treatment for a TRICARE-eligible beneficiary in an NCI-sponsored or approved protocol has obtained preauthorization for the proposed treatment before initial evaluation; and
2.2.2  Such treatments are NCI-sponsored or approved Phase I, Phase II, Phase Ill, or Phase IV protocols; and
2.2.3  The clinical trial is interventional, rather than observational, and studies a specific treatment for a specific cancer or complication of cancer.
2.2.4  The patient continues to meet entry criteria for said protocol; and
2.2.5  The institutional and individual providers are TRICARE-authorized providers.
3.0  POLICY CONSIDERATIONS
3.1  Identification of Eligible NCI-Sponsored or Approved Clinical Trials
3.1.1  NCI sponsorship of clinical trials occurs through the Cancer Therapy Evaluation Program (CTEP), Cooperative Group Studies, NCI Grants or Cancer Center Studies. Evidence of NCI sponsorship in one of these categories will be that it is identified in the NCI comprehensive database, Physician’s Data Query (PDQ), or NCI supplements to that database; formal notification of approval from The Clinical Protocol Review and Monitoring Committee; or verification from the NCI project officer; or through protocols co-sponsored by the NCI and other Federal Agencies. NCI Sponsorship may also be identified on the trial-specific web page at https://clinicaltrials.gov/.
3.1.2  An NCI-approved study refers to a clinical research study involving human participants and studying a specific intervention in which the NCI collaborates with the study sponsor to provide resources or other support towards the development of the clinical trial and/or analysis of its results. This support may include funding, design, implementation, data analysis, or reporting. NCI approval of clinical trials may be confirmed on the trial-specific web page at https://clinicaltrials.gov/ when NCI is listed as a “collaborator”.
3.1.3  Requests for treatment in clinical trials overseas must be verified as to NCI sponsorship or approval with the NCI project officer.
3.1.4  Some clinical trials are designated as multiple-phased trials (e.g., Phase I/II). Multi-phase NCI-sponsored or approved clinical trials are eligible for TRICARE coverage as long as the beneficiary is a participant in a trial phase that would normally be covered in a single-phase trial.
3.2  The DoD has no authority regarding the protocol eligibility for clinical trials not sponsored or approved by the DoD. Therefore, if a patient does not meet the protocol eligibility criteria for enrollment in a specific clinical trial, appeal rights do not apply.
3.3  Retroactive authorizations may be authorized in accordance with the provisions outlined in 32 CFR 199.4(g)(19).
3.4  Claims will be paid from the applicable underwritten Contract Line Item Number (CUN) and submitted through normal TRICARE Encounter Data (TED) system processing as required in the TRICARE Systems Manual (TSM). Special processing code CP will be used to designate the claim is associated with a cancer clinical trial.
3.5  Normal TRICARE eligibility, reimbursement, co-payments, cost-shares, deductibles, TRICARE for Life (TFL), and double coverage rules apply.
3.6  The contractor shall:
3.6.1  Ensure the provider has submitted preauthorization, which shall include the following components:
3.6.1.1  Provide the patient’s name and the last four digits of the sponsor’s Social Security Number (SSN); and
3.6.1.2  Certify the protocol is an NCI-sponsored or approved study and provide the title of the protocol, NCI number of the protocol, ClinicalTrials.gov ID of the protocol, and/or other appropriate evidence of NCI sponsorship or approval; and
3.6.1.3  Certify the patient meets all entry criteria for said protocol; and
3.6.1.4  Certify notification will be provided to the contractor if the patient becomes ineligible for the study prior to the treatment.
3.6.2  Utilize the NCl’s Comprehensive Cancer Database, known as the PDQ, to assist in determining whether a particular study meets the requirements of the cancer clinical trials benefit and whether the patient is eligible for a particular protocol. For those studies that are not listed on the PDQ, or where sponsorship or approval are unclear on the website for the clinical trial at https://clinicaltrials.gov, the contractor will work with NCI staff to verify NCI sponsorship or approval.
4.0  EXCLUSIONS
4.1  Studies that are observational, only study the pathology of a specific cancer, or that only study the impact of the cancer on population health.
4.2  Phase 0 clinical trials.
4.3  Care rendered in the NIH Clinical Center or paid in full via an NIH grant program.
4.4  Clinical trials studying treatments that even if proven would be otherwise excluded by statute or regulation (e.g., exercise programs or cosmetic procedures).
4.5  Costs associated with non-treatment research activities or services and supplies not part of routine care provided in conjunction with the clinical trial. These include, but are not limited to: data collection activities, management and analysis of the data, salaries of the research nurses, and the cost of the investigational agents or investigational methods (if used in the protocol). These research costs will also not be the responsibility of the beneficiaries participating in the clinical trials.
4.6  Any other service or supply that is provided free of charge under the investigational protocol.
4.7  Trials not meeting the requirements of this section (e.g., industry-sponsored trials that are neither NCI-sponsored nor approved).
5.0  EFFECTIVE DATE
Effective August 27, 2025. Coverage of cancer clinical trials prior to this date can be found at Section 24.1.
