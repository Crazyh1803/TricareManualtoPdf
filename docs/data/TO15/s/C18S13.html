TRICARE Operations Manual 6010.59-M, April 1, 2015
Demonstrations And Pilot Projects (Except Value-Based Initiatives)
Chapter 18
Section 13
Competitive Plans Demonstration (CPD)
Revision:  C-166, September 10, 2026
1.0  PURPOSE
The purpose of the CPD is to implement certain requirements set forth in the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2017 (NDAA FY17), Section 705, Value-Based Purchasing (VBP) and Acquisition of Managed Care Support Contracts (MCSCs) for the TRICARE Program. VBP is an emerging reimbursement strategy in the health care industry that seeks to reward better health outcomes, enhance the beneficiary’s experience of care, and reduce health care costs over time. NDAA FY17, Section 705 requires the Department to develop and implement value-based incentive programs as part of its TRICARE MCSCs. Section 705(c)(3) specifically requires the Department to “ensure that local, regional and national health plans have an opportunity to participate in competition for managed care support contracts under the TRICARE Program” and that contracts awarded to such plans incorporate, among other things, value-based reimbursement methodologies and “financial incentives for contractors and health care providers to receive an equitable share in the cost savings to the Department resulting from improvement in health outcomes for covered beneficiaries and the experience of beneficiaries in receiving health care.” The Defense Health Agency (DHA) will, through the CPD, implement the foregoing aspects of Section 705 and test and evaluate the following:
•  Competition between contractors with geographically overlapping networks.
•  Impact of a risk adjusted population based Per Member Per Month (PMPM) payment model for the improvement of access to care for covered beneficiaries.
•  Impact of a risk adjusted population based capitated PMPM payment model for the improvement of the quality of health care provided to covered beneficiaries.
•  Whether a risk adjusted population based capitated PMPM payment model will be cost neutral to the Department of Defense (DoD) per capita costs of health care provided to covered beneficiaries.
•  Impact of a risk adjusted population based capitated PMPM payment model on the improvement of health outcomes for covered beneficiaries.
•  Cost efficiencies associated with using an external Eligibility, Enrollment, and Encounter (EEE) process (carried out via the Agency’s EEE Pilot).
2.0  BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES
2.1  The CPD is a value-based initiative that seeks to improve health outcomes, maintain current health care cost profiles (i.e., achieve cost neutrality), and reduce health care cost risks to the Government. The purpose of the CPD is to test the hypothesis that a risk adjusted population based capitated PMPM payment model with competition between contractors with geographically overlapping networks will result in higher levels of efficiency, effectiveness of care, and beneficiary satisfaction than currently experienced under the traditional TRICARE MCSC construct.
2.2  The CPD objectives are:
2.2.1  Assess efficacy of having multiple contractors or overlapping networks in the same geographic area with the MCSCs and/or Uniformed Services Family Health Plan (USFHP).
2.2.2  Test the interoperability and effectiveness of the EEE Pilot to support the CPD contractors.
3.0  APPLICABILITY
The CPD is applicable to designated ZIP codes in the geographic areas designated by DHA. The CPD’s period of performance is in the CPD contract(s).
4.0  PROGRAM OVERVIEW
4.1  The CPD contractor shall deliver the TRICARE Prime option.
4.2  The CPD contractor shall provide Primary Care Managers (PCMs) through its network of providers and coordinating referrals, to the extent practicable, to other CPD network specialty providers.
4.3  The CPD contractor shall provide comprehensive health care services to all eligible CPD Prime enrollees for which the CPD contractor shall receive a monthly capitation payment.
4.4  The CPD contractor shall provide enrollees access to all CPD network primary and specialty providers in the geographic areas as designated by ZIP codes in accordance with the contract, e.g., video and virtual health.
4.5  The CPD contractor shall coordinate medical management (MM) and referral services for beneficiaries, to the extent practicable, enrolled in the CPD to include managing copayments, cost-shares, deductibles, and coinsurance.
5.0  POLICY
5.1  Eligibility
5.1.1  TRICARE Prime-eligible beneficiaries residing in the CPD designated service area may voluntarily elect to enroll in the CPD with the following exceptions: Active-Duty Service Members (ADSMs), Guard/Reserve Service Members, and beneficiaries who are eligible for Medicare are not eligible to enroll into the CPD. Beneficiaries participating in the following benefit programs are also not eligible to enroll into the CPD:
•  TRICARE For Life (TFL)
•  TRICARE Reserve Select (TRS)
•  TRICARE Reserve Retired (TRR)
•  TRICARE Young Adult (TYA)
5.1.2  Due to CPD program design and the Government’s desire to maintain beneficiary continuity of care, beneficiaries participating in, or receiving benefits under, the following special programs are not eligible to enroll into the CPD:
•  Autism Care Demonstration (ACD)
•  Extended Care Health Option (ECHO) Program
•  Wounded, Ill, and Injured Program
•  Continued Health Care Benefits Program (CHCBP)
•  Transitional Assistance Management Program (TAMP)
5.2  Benefit
5.2.1  The CPD contractor shall offer the TRICARE Prime benefit.
5.2.2  The CPD contractor shall link their CPD website to the appropriate TRICARE.mil websites for accurate TRICARE information and may provide a list on their site where benefits and costs differ, for example by listing offered value-added items and services (VAIS).
5.2.2.1  The CPD contractor shall only use VAIS with prior notification to DHA and only market the VAIS to prospective CPD enrollees with prior approval by DHA.
5.2.2.2  The CPD contractor shall not use the VAIS as an inducement or incentive for beneficiary enrollment, nor shall it claim or imply that the VAIS are recommended or endorsed by TRICARE or the DHA.
5.2.2.3  The CPD contractor shall include a disclaimer explaining value-added services are not a TRICARE benefit on any notification the CPD contractor sends to its enrollees about the availability of VAIS.
5.3  Beneficiary Liability
5.3.1  Under the CPD, beneficiary liability shall match the TRICARE Prime benefit plan with respect to required enrollment fees, copayments, Point of Service (POS) deductibles and cost-shares, and catastrophic caps, unless otherwise identified.
5.3.2  The CPD contractor shall collect medical encounter data showing services provided and report beneficiary cost-share amounts to ensure accurate tracking towards the catastrophic caps.
5.4  CPD Provider Network
5.4.1  The CPD contractor’s provider network shall be sufficiently robust to meet TRICARE Prime access-to-care (ATC) standards, as described in Chapter 5, Section 1, in the CPD designated service area.
5.4.2  The CPD contractor shall monitor and report care rendered by providers outside the CPD network. Details for reporting are identified in DD Form 1423, Contract Data Requirements List (CDRL), located in Section J of the applicable contract).
5.4.3  The CPD contractor shall educate enrollees on the use of its on-line provider network directories. The contractor’s on-line provider network directory shall include primary and specialty care providers, facilities, vendors for Durable Medical Equipment (DME), ambulance transport, and transplant.
5.5  Enrollment
5.5.1  The EEE contractor shall manage enrollment in the CPD. Beneficiaries may enroll in the CPD even if they have not previously been enrolled to, or are not currently enrolled in, TRICARE Prime. TRICARE Open Season and Qualifying Life Event (QLE) rules apply to CPD enrollment. Beneficiaries will be locked out of enrolling in the CPD if in violation of the Direct Care (DC) system/Military Medical Treatment Facility (MTF) lockouts (i.e., repeatedly accessing MTFs for other than emergency services or pharmacy benefits).
5.5.2  The CPD contractor shall have an interface with the EEE contractor for the exchange of enrollment information.
5.5.3  The CPD contractor shall communicate, collaborate, and coordinate with the EEE contractor for transition in and transition out activities.
5.5.4  The CPD contractor shall coordinate with the EEE contractor to ensure system integration and testing requirements are met within the required timeframe.
5.5.5  At a minimum, the CPD contractor shall meet with the EEE contractor monthly to discuss any systems changes or updates, unless there are issues with data exchanges between contractors that impact production, testing, and/or processing of data.
5.5.5.1  If such issues arise, the contractors shall coordinate with the Government Designated Authority (GDA) to establish more frequent meetings until the issue(s) is resolved.
5.5.5.2  Meeting topics shall include, but not be limited to, the following:
•  Reviewing the status of system connectivity and communications.
•  Issuing software enhancements.
•  Implementing system changes required for the implementation of new programs and/or benefits.
•  Reviewing data correction issues and corrective actions to be taken (e.g., research and adjustments).
•  Monitoring results of contractor testing efforts.
5.5.6  CPD and EEE enrollment processes are described in the Process Flowcharts in Section J of the contract.
5.5.7  Enrollment Fee Waiver
5.5.7.1  The Government will waive the annual TRICARE Prime enrollment fee for the initial 12 months a beneficiary is enrolled in the CPD. This is a one-time only enrollment fee waiver.
5.5.7.2  If a beneficiary disenrolls from the CPD while an enrollment fee waiver is in effect, the fee waiver will be terminated. If the beneficiary re-enrolls in the CPD before the original 12 months has passed, the fee waiver will be reinstated, ending on the date originally scheduled.
Example:  Beneficiary enrolls in CPD beginning January 1, 2025, and is given an enrollment fee waiver that ends December 31, 2025; beneficiary disenrolls from CPD in May 2025, and fee waiver is removed; beneficiary re-enrolls in CPD October 1, 2025, (within the original 12-month waiver period) and the remaining fee waiver is reinstated, ending December 31, 2025.
5.5.8  Disenrollment
5.5.8.1  An enrollee’s violation of the DC system/MTF lockouts (i.e., repeatedly accessing MTFs for other than emergency services or pharmacy benefits) could result in disenrollment of such enrollee.
5.5.8.2  A CPD enrollee shall be disenrolled, in coordination with the GDA, from the CPD when the enrollee no longer satisfies the criteria to participate in the CPD (see paragraph 5.1).
5.5.8.3  Exception for eligibility requirement to reside in a CPD designated ZIP code: If a beneficiary enrolled in CPD has an address change resulting from a ZIP code change issued by the United States Postal Service, the beneficiary remains eligible to participate in the CPD and will NOT be disenrolled. The EEE contractor will add this ZIP code to the list of eligible CPD ZIP codes in DEERS.
5.5.8.4  The CPD contractor shall, within three business days, notify the EEE contractor (for disenrollment processing purposes) of an enrollee’s disqualification from the CPD.
5.5.8.5  If a CPD enrolled beneficiary is diagnosed with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) and referred to the ACD for Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) services, they shall be disenrolled from the CPD and re-enrolled in TRICARE Prime or TRICARE Select. The diagnosis is also a QLE (as of the diagnosis date) for all family members.
5.5.8.5.1  The CPD contractor shall notify the EEE contractor of the beneficiary’s request for disenrollment processing within three business days of a beneficiary request to participate in the ACD.
5.5.8.5.2  A CPD enrollee’s diagnosis of ASD and referral to the ACD for ABA services is a QLE and family members may choose to remain enrolled to the CPD or enroll in another TRICARE health plan based on their eligibility.
5.5.8.5.3  A CPD enrollee may be eligible for ECHO enrollment and, request ECHO services for a qualifying condition per the TRICARE Policy Manual (TPM), Chapter 9 shall be disenrolled from the CPD. ECHO eligibility is a QLE and family members may choose to remain enrolled to the CPD or enroll in another TRICARE health plan based on their eligibility.
5.5.8.6  In all cases involving disenrollment of a beneficiary from the CPD, the CPD contractor shall provide the beneficiary information about other available TRICARE Plans when the event or basis for disenrollment is identified. The beneficiary’s ability to enroll in another TRICARE Plan will be contingent on the beneficiary’s eligibility for other such plans.
5.5.9  PCM Assignment
5.5.9.1  The CPD contractor shall assign all CPD enrollees a PCM.
5.5.9.2  The CPD contractor shall provide enrollees with a viable and transparent method for changing their CPD PCMs, e.g., on the CPD contractor’s website or via a telephone call with the contractor’s member services.
5.5.10  Clinical Operations (MM, Case Management (CM), Population Health (PH), Utilization Management (UM), Referral Management (RM), and Clinical Quality Management (CQM))
The CPD contractor shall ensure all care delivery and associated functions meet required TRICARE CPD reporting and performance requirements as outlined in the TRICARE Manuals and the CPD contract.
5.5.11  Nurse Advice Line (NAL)
The CPD contractor shall provide a NAL for CPD enrolled beneficiaries that is available 24 hours a day/7 days a week (24/7).
5.6  Special Programs
The CPD contractor shall ensure staff managing the interface with the EEE contractor for special and high-profile programs understand the eligibility, benefits, and benefit exclusions of these programs as articulated in the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR), Section 199, relevant TRICARE Manuals, and CPD contract requirements.
5.7  Claims and Encounters
5.7.1  The CPD is a capitated payment demonstration.
5.7.1.1  The DHA will pay the CPD contractor a capitated payment to deliver healthcare to enrolled beneficiaries.
5.7.1.2  The CPD contractor shall not submit claims to the DHA for payment.
5.7.1.3  The CPD contractor’s claims processing/encounter system shall collect medical encounter data and correctly report copay, TRICARE Prime and POS deductible, and cost-share information to the EEE contractor to be recorded on the beneficiary’s catastrophic cap in accordance with the TRICARE benefit policy as delineated in 32 CFR 199.4, CFR 199.17, CFR 199.18, as well as all applicable sections of the TPM, TRICARE Operations Manual (TOM), and TRICARE Reimbursement Manual (TRM).
5.7.1.4  Any payments collected in error by the CPD contractor (e.g., copayment collected in excess of catastrophic cap limit) shall be refunded to the beneficiary by the CPD. The CPD contractor shall track the beneficiary’s deductible and catastrophic cap status throughout the year. The CPD shall adjust any claims and copayments provided after the family has met their catastrophic cap. The EEE contractor will provide a monthly report listing beneficiaries who are over catastrophic cap in the DEERS Catastrophic Cap and Deductible (CCD) application.
5.7.2  Pharmacy claims data shall be processed, maintained, and captured by the TRICARE Pharmacy (TPharm) contractor.
5.7.3  The Government will reject all other claims by using edits in the TRICARE Encounter Data (TED) system.The contractor shall use Health Care Delivery Program (HCDP) XXXX for identification and jurisdiction of CPD claims.
5.8  Customer Service and Education
5.8.1  The CPD contractor shall proactively educate CPD eligible TRICARE beneficiaries, enrollees, and providers in the CPD-designated area by providing contractor created, DHA approved, educational and marketing materials which include links to the TRICARE website.
5.8.2  The Government will provide oversight and assistance to the CPD contractor to ensure its communication efforts effectively and accurately distribute information about the TRICARE program and CPD.
5.8.3  The contractor shall ensure differences in the CPD benefit are explained to the beneficiary before, or at, the time of enrollment. See paragraph 5.2.2 regarding marketing of VAIS.
5.8.4  The CPD contractor shall provide informative educational materials when a beneficiary enrolls, or if there are changes to how care is received, regarding how to receive specialty care, the CPD NAL, and pharmacy services. 5.8.5  The CPD contractor shall not put the last four digits of a Sponsor’s Social Security Number (SSN) on customer service and education materials sent to CPD enrollees.
6.0  EXCHANGE OF BENEFICIARY INFORMATION
The CPD contractor shall communicate, collaborate, and coordinate with the MCSCs, TPharm contractor, EEE contractor, other TRICARE contractors and DHA in order to facilitate the necessary exchange of beneficiary information.
7.0  REPORTS
The contractor shall submit DD Form 1423, CDRL, as required by Section J of the applicable contract.
8.0  EFFECTIVE DATE
Beneficiary services for the CPD shall commence with the start of 2025 open enrollment season and CPD start of health care delivery (SHCD) will begin on January 1, 2026 unless otherwise directed by the Contracting Officer (CO).
9.0  TRANSITION-IN
9.1  MANAGEMENT AND ADMINISTRATION
9.1.1  Transition Specifications (TRANSPEC) Meeting
9.1.1.1  The CPD contractor shall attend a five-business day meeting with DHA personnel within 14 calendar days following contract award.
9.1.1.2  The DHA will notify the contractor as to the exact date, length, and location of the meeting and will provide the CPD contractor with a meeting agenda prior to the TRANSPEC Meeting.
9.1.2  Interface Meetings
9.1.2.1  The CPD contractor shall meet with DHA Communications within 30 calendar days following contract award to review requirements in Chapter 11 and other DHA Communications associated activities.
9.1.2.2  The CPD contractor shall meet with DHA RM and CO within 30 calendar days following contract award to receive training in accordance with Chapter 9, Section 1 and to establish an understanding of the DHA records requirements in accordance with Chapter 9.
9.1.2.3  The CPD contractor shall conduct site visits within its geographical area of responsibility as designated by DHA.
9.1.2.4  The DHA will notify the CPD contractor as to the exact date, length, and location of all interface meeting(s) and will provide the meeting agenda prior to the meeting(s).
9.1.3  Transition Status Meetings
9.1.3.1  The CPD contractor shall participate in transition-in status meetings to provide the DHA a status or progress of all transition activities.
9.1.3.2  The DHA will notify the CPD contractor as to the frequency and location of such meetings and provide the agenda for the meeting(s).
9.1.4  Transition-In Deliverables
9.1.4.1  The CPD contractor shall deliver an initial Integrated Master Plan (IMP), in the format, and by the date, identified in the DD Form 1423, CDRL, located in Section J of the applicable contract.
9.1.4.2  The CPD contractor shall deliver an initial Integrated Master Schedule (IMS), in the format, and by the date, identified in the DD Form 1423, CDRL, located in Section J of the applicable contract.
9.1.4.3  The DHA will review and either accept or reject the initial IMP and IMS. If the DHA rejects the deliverable, then the CPD contractor shall resubmit the IMS or the IMP within 15 calendar days for the DHA’s review and acceptance.
9.1.4.4  The CPD contractor shall deliver a weekly IMP status report, as identified by DD Form 1423, CDRL, located in Section J of the applicable contract.
9.1.4.5  The CPD contractor shall deliver a weekly IMS status report, as identified by DD Form 1423, CDRL, located in Section J of the applicable contract.
9.1.4.6  The CPD contractor shall deliver a weekly risk and mitigation report, as identified by DD Form 1423, CDRL, located in Section J of the applicable contract.
9.1.5  Performance Readiness Validation (PRV) - General
9.1.5.1  The CPD contractor shall conduct a PRV to assess their performance readiness in the areas:
•  Provider Networks
•  Enrollments
•  Customer Service
•  RM
•  Claims Processing
•  MM
9.1.5.2  The CPD contractor shall provide the DHA a comprehensive briefing on the processes, results, and course of actions of each PRV assessments no later than 14 calendar days after each assessment.
9.1.6  Performance Readiness Assessment And Verification (PRAV)
The DHA will conduct PRAV activities to assess and verify the CPD contractor’s performance readiness in the areas identified in paragraph 9.1.5.
9.1.6.1  The CPD contractor shall comply with the DHA’s approach for assessment and verification of the contractor’s performance readiness in the areas identified in paragraph 9.1.5.
9.1.6.2  The DHA will provide the CPD contractor with projected PRAV dates, location(s), team structure, and specific assessment and verification methods prior to PRAV activities.
9.1.6.3  Following the completion of a PRAV activity, the DHA will provide a brief to the CPD contractor(s) on findings. If, after review, the DHA finds the CPD contractor’s performance readiness to be deficient, the CPD contractor shall submit a detailed mitigation plan no later than 10 calendar days following presentation of the DHA’s findings.
9.2  Systems Development
9.2.1  System Interface Meetings
9.2.1.1  The CPD contractor shall provide to the GDA, at the TRANSPEC meeting, the name, address, and telephone number of the technical Points of Contact (POCs) for each of the systems implementation requirements and shall ensure participation by subject matter experts in all scheduled meetings for the understanding and clarification of systems implementation requirements at the Interface Meetings listed below.
9.2.1.2  The CPD contractor shall coordinate with the GDA no later than 30 calendar days from contract award to schedule a System Interface Meeting with the DHA and EEE contractor to identify all systems interfaces and applications necessary to meet the requirements of this contract.
9.2.1.3  The GDA will collaborate with the CPD contractor as to the exact date, length, and location of all interface meeting(s), and will provide the meeting agenda prior to the meeting(s).
9.2.2  Cybersecurity Requirements
The CPD contractor shall complete and submit all required forms (e.g., System Security Plan (SSP)) in accordance with the TRICARE Systems Manual (TSM), Chapter 1.
9.2.3  Personnel Security And User Access Requirements
9.2.3.1  The CPD contractor’s Facility Security Officer (FSO) shall attend a meeting with the DHA Personnel Security Branch (PSB) within 30 calendar days after contract award to review Personnel Security requirements and procedures for requesting background checks and Common Access Cards (CACs) in accordance with the TSM, Chapter 1, Section 1.1.

9.2.3.3  The CPD contractor’s personnel shall obtain CACs from Real-Time Automated Personnel Identification System (RAPIDS) sites.
9.2.3.4  The CPD contractor shall build in the distance and appointment capacity for obtaining CACs in accordance with the transition requirements.
9.2.3.5  The CPD contractor shall use the RAPIDS locator website (https://idco.dmdc.osd.mil/idco/) for scheduling personnel who require CACs.
9.2.4  Systems Development
9.2.4.1  The DHA will, no later than 120 calendar days prior to the SHCD, test the CPD contractor’s non-claims processing systems and the telecommunications interconnections capabilities, to determine whether the systems meet the requirements of the Government and the contract.
9.2.4.2  The CPD contractor shall provide the DHA a demonstration of its non-claims processing system(s) and its telecommunications interconnections capabilities, no later than 120 calendar days prior to the SHCD.
9.2.4.3  The CPD contractor shall effectively address any corrective actions (to its non-claims processing systems and telecommunications interconnection capabilities) required by DHA no later than 30 calendar days prior to the SHCD.
9.2.5  PERFORMANCE ASSESSMENT REPORTING
The CPD contractor shall schedule initial training on the submission of CDRLs via the E-Commerce system within 30 calendar days after award. Additional training for new E-Commerce users will be provided throughout the contract upon request.
9.2.6  Transaction Testing
9.2.6.1  The CPD contractor shall comply with Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) requirements and follow CO written direction regarding transaction testing. At a minimum, testing shall include the following:
•  The CPD contractor shall conduct initial testing and compliance with all HIPAA mandated Transactions, Code Sets, and Identifiers (TCS&I) adopted by the HIPAA Final Rule in the Federal Register (see Chapter 19, Section 4, paragraph 1.0) prior to the SHCD, except for those TCS&I the DHA specifically identifies as not applying to use by the DHA.
•  The CPD contractor shall test its capability to create, send, and receive compliant transactions and shall provide written evidence (e.g., certification from a transaction testing service) of successful testing of its capabilities to create, send, and receive compliant transactions to the CO no later than 60 calendar days prior to the SHCD.
•  The CPD contractor shall test its capability no later than 60 calendar days prior to the SHCD to comply with any TCS&I Final Rule(s) adopted in the Federal Register prior to the SHCD but with required compliance date after the SHCD.
•  The CPD contractor shall provide written evidence (e.g., certification from a transaction testing service) of successful testing of their compliance with the requirements of the adopted Final Rule(s) to the contracting offices no later than 60 calendar days prior to the applicable mandated implementation date(s) of the Final Rule(s).
9.2.6.2  The CPD contractor shall make necessary corrections and re-test until a successful outcome is achieved in the event transaction compliance failures occur during testing. Failure to certify HIPAA TCS&I compliance with HIPAA named and adopted TCS&I no later than 60 calendar days prior to the SHCD may result in delay of the SHCD.
9.2.6.3  The CPD contractor shall test its capability to process standard transactions. This testing shall be “cradle-to-grave” testing from receipt of the transactions, through processing, and completion of all associated functions including creating and transmitting associated response transactions.
Note:  The TRICARE Health Plan (THP) (including business associate contractors) may be selected for a no-notice HIPAA transactions compliance audit at any time, initiated by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) through the HIPAA Compliance Review Program.

9.3.2.2  The DHA will review and either accept or reject the initial NIP. If the NIP is rejected, the incoming contractor shall resubmit the revised NIP within 15 calendar days for the DHA’s review and acceptance.

9.5.6  The CPD contractor’s training documents and response scripts shall be available for the DHA to review upon request.
9.5.7  The CPD contractor shall ensure its customer service personnel receive training on military culture and life prior to SHCD.
Note:  The DHA will provide instructions to the CPD contractor on how to obtain military culture, training, and life at the TRANSPEC Meeting.
9.5.8  The CPD contractor shall provide a risk mitigation plan to address potential call center system failure and projected call volumes including stress testing efforts. For reporting requirements, see DD Form 1423, CDRL, located in Section J of the applicable contract.
9.5.9  Customer Service PRV
9.5.9.1  The CPD contractor shall conduct an initial validation of its ability to receive calls, document calls, provide correct answers, have supervisors ready to assist with difficult calls, and perform any handoffs to other contractors. The contractor’s initial validation shall be conducted no later than 90 calendar days prior to SHCD.
9.5.9.2  The CPD contractor shall validate in its initial PRV its ability to meet the following standards. If realistic volume is not available to test, the CPD contractor shall provide a detailed plan to meet the standards.
•  Blockage rates less than 5%.
•  The AVERAGE time elapsed between INITIATED telephone ringing and connection with Automated Response Unit (ARU) shall not exceed 20 seconds.
•  On hold times less than two minutes 95% of the time.
•  Call resolution in first call 85% of the time.
•  Response accuracy 95% (responses provided by call center staff shall be accurate and complete according to the terms of the contract and all applicable TRICARE programs and policies).
9.5.9.3  The CPD contractor shall provide a call center quality monitoring report for the PRV call center validation. For reporting requirements, see DD Form 1423, CDRL, located in Section J of the applicable contract.
9.5.9.4  The CPD contractor shall provide the DHA with a comprehensive briefing on its initial PRV processes, test results, and findings within 14 calendar days of PRV completion.
9.5.9.4.1  The CPD contractor’s briefing shall summarize information that was reported in the contractor’s weekly IMP and IMS report.
9.5.9.4.2  The contractor shall brief results as validated performance against desired performance levels and contract standards and/or requirements.
9.5.9.5  The CPD contractor shall conduct a re-validation of its call center operational capabilities no later than 60 calendar days prior to SHCD.
9.5.9.5.1  The contractor shall test its ability to receive calls, document calls, provide correct answers, have supervisors ready to assist with difficult calls, and perform any handoffs to other contractor personnel with no problems.
9.5.9.5.2  The contractor shall test its capability against each of the standards referenced above and provide the results to the DHA.
9.5.9.6  The CPD contractor shall include in its validation a demonstration that its staff is fully trained.
9.5.9.7  The CPD contractor shall provide the DHA transition team with a comprehensive briefing on the processes, test results, and findings of the 60-day re-validation no later than 14 calendar days after PRV. This briefing shall summarize the information that was reported in its weekly IMP and IMS report. Results shall be briefed as validated performance against desired performance levels and contract standards and/or requirements.
9.6  Critical Processes - RM
9.6.1  The CPD contractor shall have, no later than 60 days prior to the SHCD, a fully operational RM program to facilitate the delivery of specialty care for beneficiaries in accordance with TRICARE access standards.
9.6.2  RM PRV
The CPD contractor shall validate, no later than 60 days prior to the SHCD, a fully operational RM program to facilitate the delivery of specialty care for beneficiaries in accordance with TRICARE access standards.
9.7  Critical Process - Claims Processing
9.7.1  The CPD contractor shall include in its IMP and IMS the details and schedule of activities to begin interoperability with the EEE contractor with processing/encounter data and conclude with a fully functional system at the SHCD as identified by DD Form 1423, CDRL, located in Section J of the applicable contract.
9.7.2  The CPD contractor’s claims processing/encounter system shall correctly report copays collected, TRICARE Prime and POS deductibles, and cost-share amounts to the EEE contractor for posting to the catastrophic cap (as appropriate) in accordance with the TRICARE benefit policy as described in the contract no later than 30 days prior to the SHCD.
9.7.3  Claims Processing PRV
9.7.3.1  The CPD contractor shall validate its ability to receive updates from the EEE contractor for catastrophic cap/deductible cap/deductible information as available and receive an X12 271 Health Care Eligibility Benefit response transaction with a minimum of 98% accuracy no later than 60 days prior to the SHCD. Testing shall include multiple claim types that represent a level of complexity involved in claims for TRICARE benefits. Validation shall include claims processing at the operational volume and stress testing at projected claims volumes.
9.7.3.2  The CPD contractor shall validate, no later than 60 days to the SHCD, its ability to send X12 835 (claim and advice) and X12 837 (remittance) transactions to the EEE contractor with 98% accuracy.
9.7.3.3  The CPD contractor shall validate, no later than 60 days prior to the SHCD, its ability to submit all medical, provider, and beneficiary copay, cost-share, and deductible data to the EEE contractor with 98% accuracy.
9.7.3.4  The CPD contractor shall provide validation results, to include test scenarios used, to the DHA within 14 calendar days following conclusion of the contractor’s validation.
9.8  Critical Process - Clinical Operations
9.8.1  MM
9.8.1.1  The CPD contractor shall develop and implement a MM Program that addresses all aspects of MM as described in Chapter 7, to include: CM, BH, UM, and PH.
9.8.1.2  The CPD contractor shall ensure that all aspects of its MM Program are fully operational no later than 30 calendar days prior to the SHCD.
9.8.1.3  The CPD contractor shall report the development and integration progress on an on-going basis at the transition meetings.
9.8.2  MM PRV
9.8.2.1  The CPD contractor shall begin the MM validation process by demonstrating its planned web-based application which will provide access, reporting, and detailed information to the DHA no later than 60 calendar days prior to the SHCD.
9.8.2.2  The CPD contractor shall validate that its web-based MM electronic data system can connect to the DHA Pharmacy Operations Center no later than 60 calendar days prior to the SHCD.
10.0  TRANSITION-OUT
10.1  Customer Service
10.1.1  The CPD contractor shall maintain toll-free lines and web-based customer service capabilities, accessible to the public, for 90 calendar days after the end of health care delivery (HCD) to properly respond to inquiries.
10.1.2  The CPD contractor shall maintain appropriate staff for beneficiary inquiry lines as defined in the contract.
10.1.3  The CPD contractor shall maintain adequate toll-free line coverage to ensure that the blockage rate does not exceed the blockage rate on the contractor’s most critical private or other government business access line.
10.2  Records Management
10.2.1  The CPD contractor shall develop an outgoing Records Transfer Report that details the process and timeliness of the transfer of all DHA records. For report submission requirements, see DD Form 1423, CDRL, located in Section J of the applicable contract.
10.2.2  The Government will review and set up a meeting if necessary to discuss the initial outgoing Records Transfer Report.
10.2.3  The CPD contractor shall transfer all DHA records not eligible for destruction, along with the associated metadata, to the incoming contractor or DHA Records Management in accordance with Chapter 9.
10.2.4  The CPD contractor shall transfer DHA records to the appropriate receiving party (i.e., incoming contractor or DHA Records Management) no later than the following timeline, and this timeline is applicable only for records management tracking purposes:
•  Ninety (90) calendar days prior to the end of HCD, the CPD contractor shall transfer records from the SHCD delivery to 90 days prior to the end of HCD.
•  Thirty (30) calendar days prior to the end of HCD, the CPD contractor shall transfer records from 89 days to 30 days from the end of HCD.
•  For the last 30 days of HCD, the CPD contractor shall transfer records to the incoming contractor weekly. The records shall be transferred no later than the second business day of the week.
•  Ninety (90) calendar days after the end of HCD, the CPD contractor shall deliver all remaining records.
10.2.5  The outgoing contractor shall collaborate with DHA Records Management on any records that are identified as needing to be transferred to DHA and shall follow the below requirements:
10.2.5.1  The CPD contractor shall transfer all records to DHA Records Management if there is no incoming contractor.
10.2.5.2  The CPD contractor shall request approval from the DHA Records Management Officer (RMO) prior to transferring records to the DHA RMO.
10.2.5.3  The CPD contractor shall follow electronic records transfer procedures as described in Chapter 9, Section 4 and Chapter 9, Addendum B to transfer electronic records to DHA.
10.2.5.4  The CPD contractor shall provide records in the native electronic format in which the record was created or received. Mainframe screen shots or any other system screenshots are not acceptable record formats. The acceptable formats of records are detailed in Chapter 9, Section 3.
10.2.5.5  The CPD contractor shall include required metadata files that support the accessibility, usability, searchability, and retrievability of the images. This includes any unique identifiers used to accurately retrieve electronic records or record objects.
10.2.6  The CPD contractor shall communicate, collaborate, and coordinate with the incoming contractor and DHA Records Management to report and resolve issues related to records transfer activities.
10.2.6.1  The CPD contractor shall inform the CO and DHA RMO of any records it considers proprietary.
10.2.6.2  The CPD contractor shall identify proprietary data in records within 30 calendar days of contract award.
10.2.7  The CPD contractor shall communicate, collaborate, and coordinate with the incoming contractor and DHA Records Management to conduct Proof of Concept testing to ensure the file format is acceptable and records are accessible, usable, searchable, and retrievable no later than 30 calendar days prior to the start of records transfer.
10.2.8  The CPD contractor shall communicate, collaborate, and coordinate with the GDA and the incoming contractor to provide a monthly update of transfer records activity in accordance with the outgoing Records Transfer Report, IMP and IMS.
10.2.9  Records Disposition
10.2.9.1  The CPD contractor shall comply with the provisions of Chapter 9, Section 1 for final disposition of all files and documentation.
10.2.9.2  The CPD contractor shall destroy all copies, to include backup data, in accordance with 36 CFR Part 1226, “Implementing Disposition” once the transition of records and materials has been accomplished as outlined in above paragraphs.
10.2.9.3  The CPD contractor shall not dispose of or destroy any copies or backup data prior to formal written notification from the DHA CO and DHA RMO.
10.2.9.4  The CPD contractor shall follow the provisions of Chapter 9, Section 5, Destruction of Records.
10.2.9.5  The CPD contractor shall document the removal of extra copies of records after a successful transfer of electronic records to the incoming contractor, as identified by DD Form 1423, CDRL, located in Section J of the applicable contract, Declaration of Transfer and Destruction of Records, and in accordance with 36 CFR Chapter XII, Subchapter B, Department of Defense Manual (DoDM) 5200.01, and DoD Instruction (DoDI) 8510.01.
10.2.10  Transition-Out (Phase-Out) Deliverables
10.2.10.1  No later than 90 days prior to start of Transition-Out (Phase-Out), the CPD contractor shall develop a Transition-Out (Phase-Out) plan and submit it for DHA approval (see DD Form 1423, CDRL, located in Section J of the applicable contract).
10.2.10.2  The outgoing contractor shall transfer all electronic records responsive to record freezes, to the incoming contractor, to include all associated meta data.
10.2.10.3  The outgoing contractor shall remove or redact information the contractor considers to be proprietary before transferring electronic records to the incoming contractor.
10.2.10.4  The outgoing contractor shall transfer all electronic records unable to redacted to the Government following the requirements outlined in paragraph 10.2.5 and shall determine the appropriate records transfer process and identify the specific DHA recipient office by collaborating with DHA.
10.2.10.5  During Transition-Out (Phase-Out), the CPD contractor shall submit weekly status reports documenting progress towards achieving the milestones and activities identified in the transition-out plan (see DD Form 1423, CDRL, located in Section J of the applicable contract).
