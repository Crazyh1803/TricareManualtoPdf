TRICARE Policy Manual 6010.63-M, April 2021
Medicine
Chapter 7
Section 24.5
National Institutes Of Health (NIH) Clinical Trials Studying Rare Diseases, Severely Debilitating Conditions, And Life-Threatening Conditions
Issue Date:  March 4, 2026
Authority:  32 CFR 199.4(e)(26)
Revision:  C-45, March 5, 2026
1.0  Description
On July 28, 2025, the Department of Defense (DoD) published a Final Rule (90 FR 35422) that authorizes Defense Health Agency (DHA) coverage of drugs, devices, and medical treatments or procedures provided in clinical trials sponsored or approved by National Institutes of Health (NIH) Centers and Institutes for the treatment of a rare disease or a severely debilitating or life-threatening condition. These regulatory provisions enforce an agreement between DoD and the NIH, executed September 19, 2020, both of which authorize DoD cost-sharing of routine care provided in connection with NIH sponsored or approved clinical trials and establish requirements and responsibilities for both parties.
2.0  POLICY
2.1  Coverage of services and supplies associated with clinical trial participation is authorized for those TRICARE-eligible patients selected to participate in NIH-sponsored or approved Phase I, II, Ill, and IV clinical trials studying a novel treatment or cure for a specific condition or the treatment of a currently uncontrolled symptom or aspect of that condition, provided that the condition is severely debilitating, life-threatening, or a rare disease. TRICARE will cost-share all routine medical care and testing required to determine eligibility for the clinical trial, including the evaluation for eligibility at the institution conducting the clinical trial. TRICARE will also cost-share all routine medical care required as a result of participation in eligible NIH-sponsored and approved clinical trials, including necessary follow-up care and testing that takes place after the period of active treatment on protocol is completed, unless otherwise excluded in this section or in 32 CFR 199.4(e)(26).
2.2  This includes purchasing and administering all approved pharmaceutical drugs (except for the sponsored-funded investigational agents) and all inpatient and outpatient care, including diagnostic, laboratory, rehabilitation, and home health services not otherwise reimbursed under an NIH grant program or provided in the NIH Clinical Center, if all the following conditions are met:
2.2.1  The provider seeking treatment for a TRICARE-eligible beneficiary in a clinical trial has obtained preauthorization for the proposed treatment before initial evaluation;
2.2.2  Such treatments are NIH-sponsored or approved Phase I, Phase II, Phase Ill, or Phase IV protocols;
2.2.3  The clinical trial is interventional, rather than observational, and studies a specific treatment;
2.2.4  The patient continues to meet entry criteria for said protocol;
2.2.5  The institutional and individual providers are TRICARE-authorized providers.
3.0  POLICY CONSIDERATIONS
3.1  Definitions
3.1.1  An NIH-sponsored clinical trial is defined as one in which an NIH Center or Institute initiates, funds, manages, and otherwise oversees (in whole or in part) the clinical trial.
3.1.2  An NIH-approved clinical trial is defined as one in which an NIH Center or Institute collaborates with the study sponsor to provide resources or other support towards the development of the clinical trial and/or analysis of its results; the support may include funding, design, implementation, data analysis, or reporting. Being identified as a “collaborator” on the clinicaltrials.gov website for the specific clinical trial constitutes NIH approval.
3.1.3  A rare disease is any disease or condition that has a prevalence of fewer than 200,000 persons in the United States (US).
3.1.4  A severely debilitating condition is any disease or medical condition that causes major irreversible morbidity. Severely debilitating conditions may have existing treatments that manage certain aspects of the condition, but no curative treatments for aspects of the disease that severely decrease the patient’s health and quality of life. Examples of severely debilitating conditions include Canavan Disease and Cystic Fibrosis.
3.1.5  A life-threatening condition is any disease or condition in which the likelihood of death is high unless the course of the disease is interrupted, as well as diseases or conditions with potentially fatal outcomes, where the end point of clinical trial analysis is survival. Life-threatening diseases may have existing treatments, but these treatments may increase expected life expectancy for patients with the disease while resulting in serious side effects or not curing the disease (e.g., open surgery to treat cardiovascular diseases, thrombolytics or anticoagulants to treat blood clots, insulin injections for diabetes); provide a chance of remission or disease reversal but not restore life expectancy in all patients (e.g., chemotherapy); or manage symptoms associated with the disease but not increase life expectancy (e.g., treatments for Alzheimer’s Disease). Examples of life-threatening diseases also include Hepatic Encephalopathy and Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis.
3.1.6  As used in this section, uncontrolled refers to a symptom or aspect of a specific condition that cannot be effectively managed or cured with existing medications, durable medical equipment, surgery, or other treatments or therapies. This can mean, for example, that an existing treatment has a high likelihood of harmful side effects experienced by a significant proportion of patients, including chemical dependence, elevated risk of mortality, teratogenic effects, organ damage, mental illness, or blood clots. It can also mean that the existing treatment has a relatively low probability of successfully treating a specific condition or is only palliative in nature. It does not mean that harmful side effects have the potential to occur with a specific treatment or that a specific treatment has a possibility of failure.
3.1.7  As used in this section, routine care refers to supplies, services, and treatments that TRICARE otherwise would have covered during the normal course of treatment, including costs for screening tests to determine clinical trial eligibility. Routine care includes services such as examinations, imaging, and blood tests to monitor or assess patient health during the clinical trial, and supplies such as drugs that TRICARE would cover in the absence of the clinical trial that are necessary to treat side effects associated with the treatment under study in the clinical trial, manage other aspects of a patient’s medical treatment, or are otherwise medically necessary. Routine care may differ in frequency and magnitude than care would have otherwise in the absence of the investigational treatment. For example, a patient with lung cancer might receive monthly blood tests as part of a standard treatment but require weekly tests while undergoing an investigational treatment that is part of an eligible clinical trial to monitor adverse events; these weekly tests would be considered covered routine care. Additionally, for a condition with a treatment modality that is used in both the conventional and investigational therapies, but where the investigational therapy represents a modification of the treatment, TRICARE may cost-share routine services and supplies that would have been cost-shared had the patient received the conventional therapy. For example, if a specific condition requires open surgery and the clinical trial is investigating the efficacy of a minimally invasive surgery, then TRICARE may cost-share services and supplies that would have been covered had the patient received the open surgery (e.g., anesthesia, facility charges, and post-operative care). TRICARE would neither cover the investigational device nor any other services or supplies that would be solely part of the investigational treatment or provided at no cost to beneficiaries. Consistent with the requirements of 32 CFR 199.9, TRICARE also will not cost-share care which is provided at no cost to individuals enrolled to other health plans.
3.1.8  Examples of conditions not eligible for coverage under this benefit because they are not considered severely debilitating diseases, life-threatening diseases, or rare diseases include, but are not limited to:
•  Acne
•  Acute bronchitis
•  Allergic rhinitis
•  Norovirus
•  Oral candidiasis
•  Ringworm
•  Rosacea
•  Scabies
•  Streptococcal infections
•  Tinnitus
3.2  Identification of Eligible Clinical Trials
3.2.1  There is no central repository for clinical trials eligible under this benefit. However, most clinical trials conducted in the United States, sponsored by NIH, or registered with NIH are listed in the National Library of Medicine Database at https://clinicaltrials.gov/. Trials listed in this database indicate the topic of study (e.g., the specific intervention for a specific condition and study population), as well as the study sponsor and collaborators (e.g., the National Institute of Mental Health). A clinical trial is considered to meet the requirement for NIH-sponsorship or approval if an NIH Center or Institute is listed as either a trial sponsor or a trial collaborator on the study-specific website on https://clinicaltrials.gov.
3.2.2  Clinical trials must meet all criteria in this section to be eligible under this benefit.
3.2.3  If there is any uncertainty about the eligibility of a clinical trial, then providers, contractors, and beneficiaries may contact NIH, the specific NIH Center or Institute sponsoring or collaborating on the study, or the principal investigators directly.
3.2.4  Clinical trials conducted in locations overseas are eligible for coverage. Requests for coverage of treatment in clinical trials overseas must be verified as to NIH sponsorship or approval using the procedures described in paragraph 3.6.1.
3.3  The DoD has no authority regarding the protocol eligibility for clinical trials not sponsored or approved by the DoD. Therefore, if a patient does not meet the protocol eligibility criteria for enrollment in a specific clinical trial, appeal rights do not apply.
3.4  Claims will be submitted and paid through normal TRICARE Encounter Data (TED) system processing as required in the TRICARE Systems Manual (TSM). Special processing code CQ will be used to designate the claim is associated with an NIH clinical trial eligible under this section.
3.5  Normal TRICARE eligibility, reimbursement, co-payments, cost-shares, deductibles, TRICARE for Life (TFL), and double coverage rules apply.
3.6  Contractor Responsibilities
3.6.1  The contractors shall verify that the clinical trial is NIH-sponsored or approved; studies a novel treatment or cure for a specific condition or the treatment of a currently uncontrolled symptom or aspect of that condition, provided that the condition is severely debilitating, life-threatening, or a rare disease; and otherwise meets all requirements of this section. If the contractor is unable to ascertain the sponsorship or approval status of a clinical trial on clinicaltrials.gov, then the contractor shall obtain the trial sponsorship or approval status via other means including contacting NIH, the trial investigators, and the provider submitting the prior authorization. The contractor shall not deny coverage of a beneficiary claim for participation in a clinical trial based solely on information obtained from https://clinicaltrials.gov.
3.6.2  The contractors shall verify that all services and supplies that are submitted for cost-sharing meet the requirements of this section.
3.6.3  The contractors shall ensure the provider has submitted preauthorization, which shall include the following components:
3.6.3.1  Provide the patient’s name and the last four digits of the sponsor’s Social Security Number (SSN); and
3.6.3.2  Certify that the protocol is an NIH-sponsored or approved study and provide the title of the protocol, ClinicalTrials.gov ID of the protocol, and/or other appropriate evidence of NIH sponsorship or approval; and
3.6.3.3  Certify that the patient meets all entry criteria for said protocol; and
3.6.3.4  Certify that notification will be provided to the contractor if the patient becomes ineligible for the study prior to the treatment.
3.6.4  Retroactive authorizations may be authorized in accordance with the provisions outlined in 32 CFR 199.4(g)(19).
4.0  EXCLUSIONS
4.1  Care rendered in the NIH Clinical Center or paid in full via an NIH grant program.
4.2  Phase 0 clinical trials.
4.3  Studies that are observational or that only study the pathology of the disease or condition itself or aspects of population health impacted by the disease or condition.
4.4  Clinical trials studying treatments that even if proven would be otherwise excluded by statute or regulation (e.g., exercise programs or cosmetic procedures).
4.5  Costs associated with non-treatment research activities associated with the clinical trials or care that is not routine care, as described in paragraph 3.1.7. Non-treatment research activities include, but are not limited to: data collection activities, management and analysis of the data, salaries of the research nurses, and the cost of the investigational agents or investigational methods (if used in the protocol). These research costs will also not be the responsibility of the beneficiaries participating in the clinical trials.
4.6  Any other service or supply that is provided free of charge under the investigational protocol.
4.7  Studies not meeting the requirements of this section are excluded (e.g., industry-sponsored clinical trials where no NIH Center or Instituted is listed as a sponsor or collaborator).
5.0  EFFECTIVE DATE
5.1  Effective August 27, 2025.
5.1.1  See Section 24.1 for coverage of National Cancer Institute clinical trials prior to August 27, 2025 and Section 24.3 for coverage of National Cancer Institute clinical trials on or after August 27, 2025.
5.1.2  See Section 24.4 for coverage of NIH-sponsored or approved clinical trials that study a Government-recognized infectious disease health emergency on or after August 27, 2025.
