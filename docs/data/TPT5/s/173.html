TRICARE Policy Manual 6010.63-M, April 2021
Medicine
Chapter 7
Section 24.4
Infectious Disease Health Emergency Clinical Trials
Issue Date:  March 4, 2026
Authority:  32 CFR 199.4(e)(26)
Revision:  C-45, March 5, 2026
1.0  Description
On July 28, 2025, the Department of Defense (DoD) published a Final Rule (90 FR 35422) that authorizes Defense Health Agency (DHA) coverage of drugs, devices, and medical treatments or procedures provided in clinical trials sponsored or approved by the National Institutes of Health (NIH) Centers and Institutes for the prevention or treatment of a Government-recognized infectious disease health emergency. These regulatory provisions enforce an agreement between DoD and NIH, executed September 19, 2020, both of which authorize DoD cost-sharing of routine care provided in connection with NIH sponsored or approved clinical trials and establish requirements and responsibilities for both parties.
2.0  POLICY
2.1  Coverage of services and supplies associated with clinical trial participation is authorized for those TRICARE-eligible patients selected to participate in NIH-sponsored or approved Phase I, II, Ill, and IV clinical trials studying the prevention, screening, early detection, or treatment of a Government-recognized infectious disease health emergency and associated sequelae, as defined in paragraph 3.1.3. TRICARE will cost-share all routine medical care and testing required to determine eligibility for the NIH-sponsored or approved clinical trial, including the evaluation for eligibility at the institution conducting the NIH-sponsored or approved clinical trial. TRICARE will also cost-share all routine medical care required as a result of participation in eligible NIH-sponsored or approved clinical trials, including necessary follow-up care and testing that takes place after the period of active treatment on protocol is completed, unless otherwise excluded in this section or in 32 CFR 199.4(e)(26).
2.2  This includes purchasing and administering all approved pharmaceutical drugs (except for the sponsor-funded investigational agents) and all inpatient and outpatient care, including diagnostic, laboratory, rehabilitation, and home health services not otherwise reimbursed under an NIH grant program or provided in the NIH Clinical Center, if the following conditions are met:
2.2.1  The provider seeking treatment for a TRICARE beneficiary in a clinical trial has obtained preauthorization for the proposed treatment before initial evaluation;
2.2.2  Such treatments are NIH-sponsored or approved Phase I, Phase II, Phase Ill, or Phase IV protocols;
2.2.3  The clinical trial is interventional, rather than observational, and studies a specific treatment for the infectious disease or a complication of the disease;
2.2.4  The patient continues to meet entry criteria for said protocol;
2.2.5  The institutional and individual providers are TRICARE-authorized providers.
3.0  POLICY CONSIDERATIONS
3.1  Definitions
3.1.1  An NIH-sponsored clinical trial is defined as one in which an NIH Center or Institute initiates, funds, manages, and otherwise oversees (in whole or in part) the clinical trial.
3.1.2  An NIH-approved clinical trial is defined as one in which an NIH Center or Institute collaborates with the study sponsor to provide resources or other support towards the development of the clinical trial and/or analysis of its results; the support may include funding, design, implementation, data analysis, or reporting. Being identified as a “collaborator” on the clinicaltrials.gov website for the specific clinical trial constitutes NIH approval.
3.1.3  A Government-recognized infectious disease health emergency means that the President of the United States has declared a national emergency, or the Secretary of Health and Human Services has declared a Public Health Emergency for a pandemic or epidemic that occurs as a result of an infectious disease.
3.1.4  For care rendered overseas, a Government-recognized infectious disease health emergency also includes epidemics and pandemics recognized by foreign governments and by the World Health Organization, although only NIH-sponsored or approved clinical trials in the region experiencing the Government-recognized epidemic or pandemic are eligible for TRICARE coverage.
3.1.5  As used in this section, routine care refers to supplies, services, and treatments that TRICARE otherwise would have covered during the normal course of treatment, including costs for screening tests to determine clinical trial eligibility. Routine care includes services such as examinations, imaging, and blood tests to monitor or assess patient health during the clinical trial, and supplies such as drugs that TRICARE would cover in the absence of the clinical trial that are necessary to treat side effects associated with the treatment under study in the clinical trial, manage other aspects of a patient’s medical treatment, or are otherwise medically necessary. Routine care may differ in frequency and magnitude than care would have otherwise in the absence of the investigational treatment. For example, a patient with lung cancer might receive monthly blood tests as part of a standard treatment but require weekly tests while undergoing an investigational treatment that is part of an eligible clinical trial to monitor adverse events.
3.2  Identification of Eligible Clinical Trials
3.2.1  There is no central repository for clinical trials eligible under this benefit. However, most clinical trials conducted in the United States (US), sponsored by NIH, or registered with NIH are listed in the National Library of Medicine Database at https://clinicaltrials.gov/. Clinical trials listed in this database indicate the topic of study (e.g., evaluating the efficacy of a vaccine) as well as the study sponsor and collaborators (e.g., the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases). A clinical trial is considered to meet the requirement for NIH-sponsorship or approval if an NIH Center or Institute is listed as either a trial sponsor or a trial collaborator on the study-specific website on https://clinicaltrials.gov.
3.2.2  Clinical trials must meet all criteria in this section to be eligible under this benefit.
3.2.3  If there is any uncertainty about the eligibility of a clinical trial, then providers, contractors, and beneficiaries may contact NIH, the specific NIH Center or Institute sponsoring or collaborating on the study, or the principal investigators directly.
3.2.4  Clinical trials conducted in locations overseas are eligible for coverage. Requests for coverage of treatment in clinical trials overseas must be verified as to NIH sponsorship or approval using the procedures described in paragraph 3.6.1.
3.3  The DoD has no authority regarding the protocol eligibility for clinical trials not sponsored or approved by the DoD. Therefore, if a patient does not meet the protocol eligibility criteria for enrollment in a specific clinical trial, appeal rights do not apply.
3.4  Claims will be submitted and paid through normal TRICARE Encounter Data (TED) system processing as required in the TRICARE Systems Manual (TSM). Special processing code CR will be used to designate the claim is associated with a clinical trial eligible under this section.
3.5  Normal TRICARE eligibility, reimbursement, co-payments, cost-shares, deductibles, TRICARE for Life (TFL), and double coverage rules apply.
3.6  Contractor Responsibilities
3.6.1  The contractors shall verify that the clinical trial is NIH-sponsored or approved; studies a treatment for a Government-recognized infectious disease health emergency or associated sequelae; and otherwise meets all requirements of this section. If the contractor is unable to ascertain the sponsorship or approval status of a clinical trial on https://clinicaltrials.gov, then the contractor shall obtain the clinical trial sponsorship or approval status via other means, including contacting NIH, the trial investigators, and the provider submitting the prior authorization. The contractor shall not deny coverage of a beneficiary claim for participation in a clinical trial based solely on information obtained from https://clinicaltrials.gov.
3.6.2  The contractors shall verify that all services and supplies that are submitted for cost-sharing meet the requirements of this section.
3.6.3  The contractors shall ensure the provider has submitted preauthorization, which shall include the following components:
3.6.3.1  Provide the patient’s name and the last four digits of the sponsor’s Social Security Number (SSN); and
3.6.3.2  Certify that the protocol is an NIH-sponsored or approved study and provide the title of the protocol, phase of the protocol, ClinicalTrials.gov ID of the protocol, and/or other appropriate evidence of NIH sponsorship or approval; and
3.6.3.3  Certify that the patient meets all entry criteria for said protocol; and
3.6.3.4  Certify that notification will be provided to the contractor if the patient becomes ineligible for the study prior to the treatment.
3.6.4  Retroactive authorizations may be authorized in accordance with the provisions outlined in 32 CFR 199.4(g)(19).
4.0  EXCLUSIONS
4.1  Care rendered in the NIH Clinical Center or paid in full via an NIH grant program.
4.2  Phase 0 clinical trials.
4.3  Studies that are observational or that only study the pathology of the infectious disease itself or aspects of population health impacted by the disease.
4.4  Clinical trials studying treatments that even if proven would be otherwise excluded by statute or regulation (e.g., exercise programs or cosmetic procedures).
4.5  Costs associated with non-treatment research activities associated with the clinical trials or care that is not routine care, as described in paragraph 3.1.5. Non-treatment research activities include, but are not limited to: data collection activities, management and analysis of the data, salaries of the research nurses, and the cost of the investigational agents or investigational methods (if used in the protocol). These research costs will also not be the responsibility of the beneficiaries participating in the clinical trials.
4.6  Any other service or supply that is provided free of charge under the investigational protocol.
4.7  Studies not meeting the requirements of this section are excluded (e.g., industry-sponsored clinical trials where no NIH Center or Institute is listed as a sponsor or collaborator).
5.0  EFFECTIVE DATES
5.1  Effective August 27, 2025.
5.2  The effective date for coverage of clinical trials connected to a specific infectious disease health emergency is the date the infectious disease was declared a national emergency by the President of the United States or a public health emergency by the Secretary of Health and Human Services. For infectious disease health emergencies that began prior to this final rule, the effective date is August 27, 2025.
5.3  Coverage of Coronavirus Disease 2019 clinical trials prior to April 11, 2023 is located at Section 24.2.
