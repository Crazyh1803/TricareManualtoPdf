Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 2.0  DESCRIPTION
The male genital system includes the male organs of reproduction.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.0  POLICY
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.1  Medically necessary services and supplies required in the diagnosis and treatment of disease or injury involving the male genital system are covered.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.2  A vasectomy, unilateral or bilateral, performed as an independent procedure is a covered service. (See Chapter 7, Section 2.3 for detailed policy concerning sterilization and birth control.)
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.3  For Implantable Urethral Sphincter, see Section 14.1.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.4  Diagnostic studies necessary to establish organic versus psychogenic impotence, such as lab work, a psychiatric evaluation, Doppler ultrasound, arteriography, cavernosography, cavernosometry, or electrophysiological testing may be cost-shared. (Also, see Chapter 7, Section 1.1.)
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.5  Organic impotence is defined as that which can be reasonably expected to occur following certain diseases, surgical procedures, trauma, injury, or congenital malformation. Impotence does not become organic because of psychological or psychiatric reasons.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.6  Treatment of organic impotency is covered subject to all applicable provisions of 32 CFR 199.4.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.6.1  Penile Implant.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.6.1.1  Insertion of an United States (US) Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved penile implant is covered when performed for organic impotence which has resulted from a disease process, trauma, radical surgery, or for correction of a congenital anomaly, or for correction of ambiguous genitalia which has been documented to be present at birth.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.6.1.2  Removal and reinsertion of covered penile implants and associated surgical fees may be cost-shared.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.6.2  Hormone injection, non-injectable delivery system or intracavernosal injection for the treatment of organic impotency, may be cost-shared providing the drugs are FDA approved and usage is considered generally accepted medical practice.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.6.3  External vacuum appliance for the treatment of organic impotency may be cost-shared providing the external appliance is FDA approved and usage is considered generally accepted medical practice.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.6.4  Orally administered medication for the treatment of erectile dysfunction may be cost-shared. Prior authorizations and quantity limits may be required (see Chapter 8, Section 9.1).
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.6.5  Aortoiliac reconstruction, endarterectomy, and arterial dilatations for proximal lesions for the treatment of organic impotency may be cost-shared.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.6.6  Testicular prostheses.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.6.6.1  Insertion of an FDA approved testicular prosthesis is covered when performed following disease, trauma, injury, radical surgery, or for correction of a congenital anomaly, or for correction of ambiguous genitalia which has been documented to be present at birth.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.6.6.2  If the initial testicular prosthesis surgery was for an indication covered or coverable by TRICARE, treatment of complications may be covered following reconstruction (including prosthesis removal and reinsertion) regardless of when the reconstruction was performed. Complications that may result following removal and reinsertion of prostheses are covered.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.6.6.3  If the initial testicular prosthesis surgery was for an indication not covered or coverable by TRICARE, implant removal may be covered only if it is necessary treatment of a complication which represents a separate medical condition. See Section 1.1.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.7  Infertility testing and treatment, including correction of the physical cause of infertility may be cost-shared. Hypothalamic disease, pituitary disease, disorders of sperm transport, disorders of sperm motility or function, and/or sexual dysfunction may cause male infertility. Diagnostic Services may include semen analysis, hormone evaluation, chromosomal studies, immunologic studies, special and sperm function tests, and/or bacteriologic investigation. Therapy may include, but is not limited to, hormonal treatment, surgery, antibiotics, administration of Human Chorionic Gonadotropin (HCG), and/or radiation therapy, depending upon the cause.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.8  Sex gender change and intersex surgery (Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) codes 55970 and 55980) is limited to surgery performed to treat ambiguous genitalia which is documented to have been present at birth.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.9  Medically necessary reversal of surgical sterilization for the treatment of a disease or injury such as intractable chronic scrotal pain or post-vasectomy pain (CPT codes 55400, 54900, and 54901) may be cost-shared.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 3.10  Effective April 14, 2016, prostate saturation biopsy for men at risk for prostate cancer, with one previous negative biopsy, with abnormal Digital Rectal Exam (DRE), with elevated or rising Prostate-Specific Antigen (PSA) levels and/or abnormal findings on previous biopsies (CPT code 55706) is covered.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 4.0  EXCLUSIONS
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 4.1  Penile implants and related services when performed for psychological impotence, sex gender change surgery, or such other conditions as gender dysphoria.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 4.2  Testicular prosthesis and related services when performed for sex gender change surgery or such other conditions as gender dysphoria.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 4.3  Therapy for sexual dysfunctions or inadequacies (see Chapter 7, Section 1.1).
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 4.4  Arterial revascularization for distal lesions and venous leakage when treatment is for organic impotency.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 4.5  All services and supplies directly and indirectly related to surgical treatment (i.e., sex gender change), except when performed to correct ambiguous genitalia, which is documented to have been present at birth (CPT codes 55970 and 55980).
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 4.6  Reversal of surgical sterilization (CPT codes 54900, 54901, and 55400), except as stated in paragraph 3.9Deleted in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 4.9.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 4.7  Cryosurgery for prostate metastases M or N is unproven.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 4.8  Electroejaculation (CPT code 55870).
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 4.9  Prophylactics (condoms).
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 4.10  Over-The-Counter (OTC) spemicidal products.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 4.11  Penile Vibratory Stimulation (PVS) devices, such as Ferticare Personal 2 medical vibrator.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 4.12  High-Intensity Focused Ultrasound (HIFU) for the primary treatment of localized prostate cancer (Deleted in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: Healthcare Common Procedure Coding System (HCPCS) Added in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: CPT code Deleted in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: C9747 Added in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 55880) is unproven.
Modified in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 4.13  HIFU for the salvage treatment of localized, radio-recurrent prostate cancer (Deleted in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: HCPCS Added in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: CPT code Deleted in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: C9747 Added in Revision 49, April 21, 2026: 55880) is unproven.
