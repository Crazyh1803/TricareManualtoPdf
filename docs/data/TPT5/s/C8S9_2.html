TRICARE Policy Manual 6010.63-M, April 2021
Other Services
Chapter 8
Section 9.2
Medical Claims That Include Drugs And Biologics
Issue Date:  September 4, 2026
Authority:  32 CFR 199.2(b), 32 CFR 199.4(b)(2)(v), (b)(3)(iii), (b)(5)(v), (d)(3)(vi), (e)(11)(i), 32 CFR 199.5(d)(12); and 32 CFR 199.17
Revision:  C-56, September 4, 2026
1.0  DESCRIPTION
This policy describes the TRICARE policy for medical claims that include drugs and biologics that are administered by a TRICARE authorized provider (including Home Health Agencies (HHAs)), and infusion or injectable drug therapy, even if self-administered, delivered in the home, when the drug is unavailable from the TRICARE Pharmacy (TPharm) program. Self-administered drugs and biologics provided by a retail pharmacy, mail order pharmacy, or Market/Military Medical Treatment Facility (MTF) pharmacy are described in Section 9.1.
2.0  POLICY
2.1  Jurisdiction
Claims for drugs and biologics not appropriate for self-administration (e.g., injectables), certain “take-home” drugs provided by TRICARE authorized providers (e.g., following an inpatient stay), and certain injectable/infused drugs and biologics delivered in the home are the responsibility of the contractor administering the medical benefit (i.e., the Managed Care Support Contractors (MCSCs), TRICARE Overseas Program (TOP), TRICARE for Life (TFL) program, and the Designated Providers (DPs)).
2.2  Applicability
This policy applies to medical claims with the following drugs and biologics:
2.2.1  Legend drugs and vitamins, including prenatal vitamins. Legend vitamins may be cost-shared only when used as a specific treatment of a medical condition. In addition, prenatal vitamins that require a prescription in the United States (U.S.) may be cost-shared.
2.2.2  Drugs and biologics grandfathered by the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act of 1938. Drugs and biologics grandfathered by the Federal Food, Drug and Cosmetic Act of 1938 may be cost-shared as if U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved.
2.2.3  Drugs and biologics approved by the FDA, including immunizations and vaccines. See Chapter 7, Sections 2.1, 2.2, and 2.5 for preventive and well-child care.
2.2.4  Insulin and related supplies for known diabetics, even if a prescription is not required by state law.
2.2.5  Infusion or injectable drugs and biologics appropriate for self-administration in the home, but that are unavailable from the TPharm program (see paragraph 2.6).
2.3  Coverage And Cost-Sharing
The drugs and biologics listed in paragraph 2.2. may be cost-shared under this policy when:
2.3.1  The drug or biologic is approved for marketing by the FDA (if approval is required), or the drug or biologic has an FDA Emergency Use Authorization (EUA). Coverage of a drug or biologic ends when the EUA ends unless the device is otherwise approved or cleared by the FDA;
2.3.2  The drug or biologic is prescribed by a provider, acting within the scope of his/her license, for its labeled indication;
2.3.3  The drug or biologic is furnished by a provider in accordance with all applicable state laws and licensing requirements;
2.3.4  The drug or biologic is medically or psychologically necessary, or is being provided in accordance with well-child or preventive care benefits; and
2.3.5  The drug or biologic is not otherwise excluded.
2.4  Off-Label Uses Of Drugs And Biologics
Approval for off-label uses of drugs and biologics, as defined by the TRICARE Regulation at 32 CFR 199.2, reimbursed by the medical program shall be provided by the contractor. The contractor shall provide approval for the reimbursement of off-label uses when the off-label use is medically necessary and demonstrations from medical literature, national organizations, or technology assessment bodies show that the off-label use of the drug or biologic is safe, effective and in accordance with nationally accepted standards of practice in the medical community, unless the off-label use is specifically prohibited by the TRICARE Policy Manual. If the drug/biologic is FDA approved and the off-label use is medically necessary, supported by medical literature identified by the contractor, which indicates the drug is nationally accepted as standard practice, and is not otherwise excluded, the contractor may approve the cost-sharing for the off-label drug. Drugs provided by the TOP shall continue to follow the policies established in Chapter 12, TRICARE Operations Manual (TOM), Chapter 24, and the TOP contract.
2.5   Orphan Drugs
Drugs and biologics with FDA “orphan drug” designation and marketing approval may be cost-shared when used in the treatment of a rare disease or condition, as defined in 32 CFR 199.2.
2.6  Infusion Or Injectable Drug Therapy Delivered In The Home
When injectable and infusion drug therapy are medically necessary, and delivery and administration in the home is appropriate the contractor shall provide prior authorization of injectable or infused drugs to a TRICARE authorized pharmacy in order for the drugs and biologics to be fulfilled under the pharmacy benefit pursuant to Sections 9.1 and 20.1.
2.7  Reimbursement for drugs and biologics on medical claims is described in the TRICARE Reimbursement Manual (TRM), and is dependent on the provider type, beneficiary category, and presence of Other Health Insurance (OHI). See the TRM for more information.
2.8  The policy regarding medically necessary food can be found at Section 7.1.
3.0  UTILIZATION MANAGEMENT
3.1  Utilization management is the responsibility of the contractor with responsibility for the venue distributing the pharmaceuticals. Should another contractor require data about pharmaceutical prescribing practices of clinicians or the pharmaceuticals prescribed to a patient, the contractor requiring the information may submit a request for data to the Defense Health Agency (DHA) Contracting Officer (CO). The requesting contractor shall commit to paying all costs associated with retrieving and providing any data. No contractor will be required to develop or provide data that are not available in the contractor’s data warehouse.
3.2  The contractor shall screen prescription claims for potential over-utilization and substance abuse in accordance with the TOM, Chapter 28.
3.3  Beneficiaries will be required to designate a prescriber for managing all prescriptions of Drug Enforcement Agency (DEA) Schedule II-V medications. Beneficiaries will be informed that any prescription written by other than the selected provider shall be denied authorization for dispensing through network retail pharmacies and, additionally, that any TRICARE claim for prescriptions filled by a non-network retail pharmacy will be denied reimbursement. This process will be coordinated between the contractor, the pharmacy contractor, and the Pharmacy Operations Center (POC). See Section 9.1.
Note:  Beneficiaries are entitled to benefits by law. Beneficiaries cannot be sanctioned to preclude them from seeking benefits for medical care which is appropriate and medically necessary.
4.0  EXCLUSIONS
4.1  Drugs and biologics prescribed or furnished by a member of the patient’s immediate family or a person living in the beneficiary’s or sponsor’s household.
4.2  Drugs and biologics, including compounded preparations, that are available over the counter.
4.3  Investigational drugs and biologics with FDA “Group C” designation have reproducible efficacy in one or more specific tumor types. Such a drug has altered or is likely to alter the pattern of treatment of the disease, and can be safely administered by properly trained physicians without specialized supportive care facilities. TRICARE may not cost-share use of Group C-designated drugs and biologics, because authorization for Group C distribution for a specific indication is not equivalent to formal FDA approval for that indication. Medical care related to the use of Group C-designated drugs and biologics may be cost-shared only when the care would have been provided in the absence of the use of the Group C-designated drug.
4.4  Orphan drugs and biologics without marketing approval, but which are made available on a compassionate-use basis, may not be cost-shared.
4.5  Under the FDA treatment Investigational New Drug (IND) regulations enacted in 1987, drugs and biologics that are in controlled clinical trials can be provided outside those trials to treat patients with serious or immediately life-threatening diseases for which no comparable or satisfactory alternate therapy exists. TRICARE may not cost-share treatment INDs because they have not received FDA marketing approval. However, medical care related to the use of treatment INDs may be cost-shared when the patient’s medical condition warrants their administration and the care is provided in accordance with generally accepted standards of medical practice.
5.0  EFFECTIVE DATES
5.1  Labeled uses: the date of FDA approval for the specific indication.
5.2  Off-labeled uses: the date that medical literature as described in paragraph 2.4 establishes the safety and efficacy of the drug for that specific use.
5.3  Orphan drugs and biologics: the date of FDA marketing approval.
5.4  Use of FDA EUA is effective on July 21, 2004. The effective date for coverage of a drugs and biologics with FDA EUA is the date of FDA authorization.
